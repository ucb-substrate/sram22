Signal pins have landing shapes at the macro edge, and power pins span the block.
Their locations are taken from the
LEF abstract. Use the explorer below to find any pin on the real
<code>{pins.macro}</code> layout.

- **Click a pin group** (e.g. `addr`, `din`) to highlight all of its bits and
  zoom to fit them.
- **Click an individual bit** (e.g. `dout[5]`) to locate exactly that pin.
- **Scroll** to zoom and **drag** to pan; **Reset view** returns to the full
  macro. You can also filter the list with the search box.
- Tick **Show power grid** to overlay the `vdd`/`vss` met2 straps.

<PinExplorer />

:::note[What you're looking at]

Signal pins (`clk`, `rstb`, `ce`, `we`, `wmask`, `addr`, `din`, `dout`) are
small **met1** rectangles along the **bottom edge** of the macro — those are
the discrete landing pads a router connects to, and their coordinates and
shapes come straight from the macro's `.lef`. `vdd` and `vss` are not point
pins but a **met2 power grid** of hundreds of straps spanning the whole block,
so they are kept off the pin list and shown only via the **Show power grid**
toggle.

:::

This macro measures **{pins.um_width.toFixed(2)} × {pins.um_height.toFixed(2)} µm**. Remember that it is
[placed rotated 90°](/docs/orientations/), so in a real floorplan the pin edge
runs vertically, and you connect your power distribution network to the `vdd`/`vss`
met2 straps.
