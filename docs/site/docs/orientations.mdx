:::warning[Read this before placing a macro]

SRAM22 macros are designed to be **placed rotated by 90°**. There are exactly
**four legal orientations**: R90, R270, and the mirrored variants of each.
Placing a macro at R0 or R180 is not supported.

:::

The macro is generated in a landscape "as-drawn" frame, but its pin access,
power straps, and internal routing assume it will be rotated a quarter turn when
placed. As a result, only the 90°-rotated placements — and their mirror images,
which the place-and-route tool may use to abut macros or align power — are
valid.

## The four legal orientations

<OrientationDiagram />

In place-and-route orientation codes:

| Orientation | DEF / LEF code | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R90 | `W` | Rotate 90° |
| R270 | `E` | Rotate 270° |
| R90 + mirror | `FW` | Rotate 90°, then flip |
| R270 + mirror | `FE` | Rotate 270°, then flip |

The macro's LEF declares `SYMMETRY X Y R90`, which tells the placer these
mirrored/rotated variants are equivalent and legal to use.

## Why it matters

- **Pin edge.** After a 90° rotation the signal pin edge (normally the bottom
  edge on **met1**) runs vertically. Route channels and pin access must be
  planned for the rotated edge.
- **Power.** The `vdd`/`vss` straps on **met2** likewise rotate; align your
  power grid to the rotated straps.
- **Abutment.** The mirrored variants (`FW`, `FE`) let you place two macros
  back-to-back with aligned power and pins.

## In the flows

Both tutorials fix the macro in a legal orientation explicitly:

```tcl
# OpenROAD
place_cell -inst_name u_mem -origin {120.0 80.0} -orientation R90 -status FIXED

# Innovus
place_inst u_mem 120.0 80.0 R90 -fixed
```

Constrain (or check) the macro placer so it cannot choose R0/R180. See the
[OpenROAD](/docs/tutorial/openroad/) and [Cadence](/docs/tutorial/cadence/) tutorials for
the surrounding flow.
